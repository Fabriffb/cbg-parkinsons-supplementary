Computational Assessment of the Haptenization Potential of Tattoo Ink Compounds through DFT and Molecular Docking with Human Serum Albumin

A computational investigation of the electronic reactivity and predicted protein-binding interactions of benzo[a]pyrene (BaP), (+)-anti-benzo[a]pyrene diol epoxide ((+)-anti-BPDE), and Pigment Red 170.

Project overview

This study combines density functional theory (DFT) calculations and molecular docking to explore electronic reactivity and predicted interactions between selected tattoo-ink-related compounds and human serum albumin (HSA).

BaP, its reactive metabolite (+)-anti-BPDE, and Pigment Red 170 were investigated using electronic descriptors, including frontier molecular orbitals, the HOMO–LUMO gap, chemical potential, hardness, electrophilicity, Fukui functions, and molecular electrostatic potential. Docking with AutoDock4, AutoDock Vina, and DOCK6 was used to examine predicted binding poses and non-covalent protein–ligand interactions.

Interpretation note: conventional docking predicts non-covalent binding poses and scores; it does not by itself demonstrate covalent adduct formation or immunogenicity. These results should be interpreted as computational evidence relevant to exploring haptenization potential.

Computational methods

Electronic structure
DFT geometry optimization

B3LYP-D3(BJ)/6-31+G(d,p); frequency calculations used to verify optimized structures as local minima.

Reactivity analysis
Electronic descriptors

HOMO/LUMO energies, HOMO–LUMO gap, chemical potential, chemical hardness, electrophilicity, Fukui functions, and molecular electrostatic potential.

Protein–ligand modeling
Molecular docking

Human serum albumin was used as the receptor. AutoDock4, AutoDock Vina, and DOCK6 were used to examine predicted poses and binding interactions.
